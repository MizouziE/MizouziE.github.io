# The Trial

Josef K. is arrested on the morning of his thirtieth birthday and never finds out the charge. The court system is run from attics and back rooms, the lawyers are useless in a way that feels almost procedural rather than incidental, and every attempt to engage with the process draws him further into it. The parable of the man waiting at the door of the Law sits near the end and is more or less the entire book compressed into two pages. Kafka never finished it, left instructions for the manuscript to be burnt, and Max Brod published it anyway.

I despised the main character, even though that's kind of the point, and that made this a painful read for me so I fail to see why this work is revered so highly. It feels like it spends far too much effort pointing at how bad the system is, when a book like [The Stranger](/tldr/the-stranger/) by Camus manages to convey that point in a much more humanised way. I found myself muttering "oh, shut up" a lot throughout this because it was mostly watching a man-child complain about an unfair system.
